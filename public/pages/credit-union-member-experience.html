The Challenge

A six-week gap between a member's feedback and a manager's response.

First Credit Union's member and employee feedback lived in separate tools, on separate schedules, with no way to route a bad experience to the person who could fix it.

hourglass_top

A six-week reporting cycle

By the time a quarterly report reached a branch manager, the member who left the feedback had already moved on, or moved their account.

call_split

Member and employee feedback, split apart

Two separate tools meant two separate stories. No one could see how a branch's employee experience connected to its member experience.

person_off

No owner, no follow-up

Feedback arrived in a shared inbox with no assigned owner. Without a name attached, follow-up quietly never happened.

See it in action

Member and employee feedback, one view.

Every response is tagged to the journey stage that produced it, whether that's a loan close, a branch visit, or a new hire's first ninety days, so leadership sees the whole relationship, not two disconnected numbers.

The Solution

Four changes that closed the gap

bolt

Event-triggered surveys

Surveys fire automatically after a loan closes, a branch visit ends, or an account opens, while the interaction is still fresh.

alt_route

Alerts routed to branch managers

A low score routes straight to the branch manager responsible, with full context, instead of sitting in a shared inbox.

Member and employee feedback, one view

Both programs run in the same platform with separate workspaces and one leadership view that connects the two stories.

Journey stage mapping

Every response is tagged to the stage of the member relationship it came from, so you know exactly where to focus.
