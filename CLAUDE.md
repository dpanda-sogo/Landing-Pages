# Proven hero pattern — reuse for future landing pages

This hero layout has been confirmed to convert well in production. Default to it
for new landing page heroes unless the user specifies a different structure.

**Headline formula:** "You've Never Had A [Old Problem] Problem. You've Had An
[Real Problem] Problem." — second sentence's key phrase in the accent/teal color.
(Same rhetorical device used elsewhere in this repo, e.g. "You Don't Have a
Customer Feedback Problem. You Have an Action Problem.")

**Subhead formula:** one sentence naming the transformation framework as a short
named sequence of 3-4 steps, e.g. "Sogolytics turns [raw data type] into
board-ready action — Listen, Understand, Act, Prove — in weeks, not semesters."
Always end on a fast-vs-slow timeframe contrast.

**Layout (two columns, dark gradient background with a subtle grid overlay):**
- Left: Sogolytics logomark (two small stacked dots) + wordmark, top-left.
  Headline, subhead, then a photo of a real person looking overwhelmed/stressed
  at a laptop (the "before" state) with cool blue lighting and a dot-grid
  decorative pattern behind it.
- Floating stat cards positioned around/over the photo (4 cards): each a small
  dark rounded card with a label and one glanceable metric — mix formats across
  the four cards (a percentage ring/gauge, a "N days" callout, a progress bar
  with %, a star rating). Use metrics specific to the page's vertical.
- Right: white "Book a Personalized Demo" card — H3 headline, a "Prefer to talk
  now? Call a Sogolytics expert at [phone number]" line, then a short form
  (Name, Email, Phone (optional), Organization Name) and a full-width yellow
  pill submit button with a black circular arrow icon on the left.

This is a refinement of the hero-photo-wrap + floating-metric-cards + hero-form-
card pattern already used on the K-12 and Credit Union pages in this repo —
when building the next one, match this exact refined version (rounded gauge
rings, the phone-call alternative line in the form card, the dot-grid photo
decoration) rather than the earlier, plainer version.
